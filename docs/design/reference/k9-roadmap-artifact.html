K9 Training Roadmap · Year One

COMMANDCURRICULUM

A structured 12-month command development plan for your working-breed female puppy.

Belgian Malinois Mix Dutch Shepherd Mix Female · High Drive
Obedience
Protection / Bite Sport Foundation
Advanced / Working
Socialization & Life Skills
8–12 Weeks
Foundation & Imprinting
Short 3–5 min sessions, positive reinforcement only. Build name recognition and focus first.
— Obedience
NAME
Eye contact = reward. Foundation of all commands.
SIT
First formal position. Lure with treat over nose.
NO / AH-AH
Interruption marker. Keep calm, redirect.
YES / GOOD
Reward marker word. Must be consistent.
COME
Recall foundation. Never punish a puppy for coming.
OFF
Four paws on floor. Important early habit.
— Socialization & Life Skills
CRATE
Crate = safe den. Never use as punishment.
GENTLE
Soft mouth for taking treats. Critical for this breed.
Trainer's NoteMalinois and Dutch Shepherds imprint rapidly at this stage. Expose her to surfaces, sounds, people, and handling (ears, paws, mouth) daily. Socialization IS training right now.
3–4 Months
Core Obedience
Build the 6 essential commands with duration, distance, and distraction. Introduce leash skills.
— Obedience
DOWN
Full prone position. Builds impulse control.
STAY
Duration + distance. Build 3 D's gradually.
PLACE
Go to designated mat/bed and stay. Very useful command.
HEEL
Loose-leash walking at your left side.
LEAVE IT
Ignore object on cue. Safety command.
DROP IT
Release item from mouth on command.
WAIT
Pause at threshold. Door/gate manners.
— Socialization & Life Skills
LOAD UP
Enter vehicle on command.
KENNEL
Enter crate/run on cue (different from crate at home).
Trainer's NoteBegin introducing a tug toy as a reward for drive-building. For working breeds, prey drive is a powerful training currency. Keep sessions under 10 min — end on success, always.
4–6 Months
Solidifying & Proofing
Generalize commands across all environments. Begin introducing handler focus and engagement work.
— Obedience (Proofing)
FRONT
Recall to sit directly in front of handler.
FINISH
Return to heel position from front.
FREE / BREAK
Release from all commands. Important for mental rest.
STAND
Stand still for examination. Vet/grooming essential.
— Protection Foundation (Prey Drive Only)
TUG
Engage tug on command. Channel drive purposefully.
OUT
Release bite/tug on command. Foundation of all protection work.
WATCH
Eyes on handler. Engagement / focus command.
Trainer's Note"OUT" (release) must be bomb-proof before any protection work advances. A dog that won't release on command is not a protection dog — it's a liability. Practice it hundreds of times with the tug.
6–9 Months
Intermediate & Drive Development
Adolescence phase — be patient and consistent. Drive work deepens. Begin bark/alert training if protection is the goal.
— Obedience
GO TO BED
Long-duration place command. 20–30 min stays.
BACK
Move backward on command. Spatial awareness.
SPIN / TURN
Directional spins. Builds body awareness & bonding.
UNDER
Go under table/bench. Public access skill.
— Protection / Alert Training
SPEAK / ALERT
Bark on command. Foundation of perimeter alerting.
QUIET
Cease barking on command. Equally important as speak.
GUARD
Alert posture at location. Hold and bark, no bite yet.
WITH ME
Handler-focused movement in protection context.
Trainer's NoteAdolescence (5–9 months) is often the hardest phase — she may "forget" known commands. This is normal. Stay consistent. Do NOT start formal bite work before 9–12 months and only with a professional decoy/trainer.
9–12 Months
Advanced & Working Level
Refine everything. If pursuing protection sport (IGP/Schutzhund) or personal protection, work with a certified trainer/club.
— Advanced Obedience
HEEL OFF-LEASH
Competition-level heeling without leash.
LONG DOWN
Remain in down while handler is out of sight.
DIRECTED SEND
Go to a marked location on hand signal.
RETRIEVE
Fetch and return to front with hold. IGP foundation.
JUMP
Clear obstacle on command.
— Personal Protection (Trainer-Guided Only)
FASS / GET IT
Engage threat on command. Must only be with trainer.
OUT
Immediate release — re-proof constantly.
PASS
Ignore a person/dog that is NOT a threat.
SIDE / COVER
Position beside/behind handler for escort work.
SEARCH
Search a defined area for a hidden person.
Trainer's NoteAt 1 year she's still maturing mentally — Malinois and Dutch Shepherds don't fully mature until 2–3 years. Think of year one as building a world-class foundation, not completing the structure. She'll reward every hour you invest.
⚠️ Important: Protection/bite commands should only be trained under the supervision of a certified protection dog trainer or IGP/Schutzhund club. Improper bite training on high-drive working breeds creates dangerous, unpredictable dogs. All obedience commands should be rock-solid before any protection work begins. Consult a professional trainer experienced with Malinois or Dutch Shepherds specifically.
